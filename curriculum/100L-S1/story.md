## The set-up

You have just started as a junior developer at **Tiny Lamp Studio**, a two-person web studio near
campus. At the Campus Fair (Assignment 02), the studio met its newest client: **Mama Titi**, who runs a
canteen and sells through **CampusChop** (`campuschop.example`, from Assignment 01). Her files are a
mess, her order slips add up wrong, and she wants a website of her own.

Each week she brings a problem. Each problem needs that week's skill. While you work for her, Tolu
tells you to build your own **portfolio** with the same skills, because that is what the curriculum's
labs deliver.

## Writing rules for the story

- **The story serves the concept.** Each beat opens with a story problem (two to four sentences), then
  teaches the idea. If a story moment doesn't lead to a concept or a task, cut it.
- **Teach only what students know so far.** Mama Titi can't ask for something the programme hasn't
  taught yet, unless it is that week's cliffhanger.
- **Plain English first.** Many readers read English as a second language. Keep local flavour (naira,
  WhatsApp, campus places, food), and never put Pidgin in instructions, definitions or questions.
- **Never put graded answers in the story.** Tolu asks questions about graded work, not answers them.
- **Mama Titi's files are practice files.** They live in each week's practice kit. Nothing in the story
  is submitted, and nothing a student submits goes into the story.
