## 1. Find the shape of a page · 8 minutes

**(paper)** Every useful document has a shape before it has a look. Here is part of a notice from a
departmental noticeboard, typed with no formatting:

```text
Department of Computing
Notice to all 100L students
Practical sessions
Bring your laptop and charger
Bring your student ID
Arrive ten minutes early
How to collect your lab pass
Fill in the form at the office
Pay at the bursary
Show your receipt and collect the pass
Lab times
Group A Monday 10 am Lab 2
Group B Tuesday 2 pm Lab 1
```

1. Copy it into your notebook, or write on this page. Circle the **one main title**.
2. Underline the **section titles**.
3. Mark each line that is an item in a list with a dot (**•**). If the order of a list matters, number
   its lines instead.
4. The last two lines hold the same three kinds of fact. Draw them as a grid with a heading for each
   column.

**Record:** main title __________ · how many sections __________ · which list needs numbers
__________ · my column headings __________ / __________ / __________ / __________

## 3. Get ready for your portfolio · 10 minutes

Your portfolio is a page about you and your work. You start it in class.

1. **(paper)** Write three or four **section titles** your page could have, such as a section about you,
   what you can do, and what you have learnt so far. Use your own words.
2. **(laptop or phone)** Choose **one image** you are happy to put on your page. It doesn't need to be
   your face: a photo of something you made, a drawing, or a screenshot of last week's Hello World page all
   work. Use only an image you made or have the right to share.
3. **(laptop)** Put the image in a folder you can find. Check its full name, including the extension you
   learnt about in week 4, such as `.jpg` or `.png`. If you can, rename it in lowercase with no spaces,
   such as `my-hello-page.png`.

**Record:** my section titles __________ · my image file name __________

**No laptop?** Do step 1 on paper, and describe in words the image you would use.

> **Offline route:** Tasks 1, 3 and 4 need no internet. For Task 2, use this summary. An HTML **element**
> is usually an **opening tag** such as `<p>`, some content, and a **closing tag** such as `</p>`. The
> **tag** is just the label in angle brackets; the **element** is the whole thing. Elements can sit inside
> other elements, which is called **nesting**. A few elements, such as `<img>`, have no content and no
> closing tag. **Attributes** add information inside the opening tag, such as `src="photo.jpg"`. A
> **table** holds data in rows and columns, such as prices or timetables. Don't use tables to arrange a
> page: that is CSS's job, and layout tables confuse screen readers.
